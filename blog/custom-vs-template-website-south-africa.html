Custom vs Template: What You're Actually Paying For

By PW de Bruyn 4 August 2026 6 min read

Somewhere in every website conversation, someone says the word "custom" as though it settles the argument. It does not. Custom is not a quality level — it is a description of where the work started. Plenty of custom sites are worse than a well-set-up template, and plenty of template sites bring in work for years. What you are really choosing between is how many hours go into decisions specific to your business.

Short answer

A template build starts from a ready-made design and adapts it; a custom build designs the layout around your business from scratch. The gap is mostly hours, which is why custom costs more. Most South African small businesses do best with a hybrid: a proven framework, customised where it earns money.

What is the real difference?

A template — or theme — is a finished design somebody else built, which your designer fills with your logo, colours, photos and words. A custom build starts with a blank page and designs the layout around what your business needs a visitor to do.

Almost everything else people believe about the difference is wrong. Templates are not inherently slow, insecure or bad for search. Custom is not inherently faster or more effective. A rushed custom build by someone who never asked what your customers want is worse than a carefully set-up theme with excellent copy.

The honest distinction is how many decisions were made about your specific business. On a template build, the decisions about page structure, hierarchy and where the buttons go were made in advance, by someone who has never met you, for a generic business in your category. Often that is fine. Sometimes it is exactly wrong — a theme designed for a boutique will bury the phone number that an emergency plumber needs at the very top.

In practice, most professional South African builds sit in the middle. A studio starts from a framework it knows well, then rebuilds the pages that carry commercial weight: the home page, the main service page, and the enquiry flow. That is the hybrid, and it is usually the best value on the table.

Two costs that catch people out. Templates carry licence renewals — themes and premium plugins that bill annually, and occasionally a theme developer simply stops updating, which eventually forces a rebuild. Custom carries concentration risk: a bespoke site built on one person's unusual setup can be difficult for anyone else to maintain. Ask, either way, what happens in three years.

When is a template genuinely the right call?

More often than the industry likes to admit.

You are new and still learning what you sell. Spending heavily on a custom structure before you know which service actually pays the bills is guessing with a bigger budget. Launch, watch, rebuild on evidence.

Your competitive edge is not the website. If customers pick you on price, availability or proximity, a clean and fast template with strong copy will not hold you back. A tyre fitter open on Sundays wins on being open on Sundays — the site's job is to make that impossible to miss.

Budget is genuinely tight. A good template build that goes live beats a custom build you cannot finish. Half-built sites do not rank and do not sell.

You need it this month. Seasonal deadlines are real. Live and imperfect earns more than perfect and late.

If you take the template route, spend the money you saved on words and photographs. Real photos of your actual team and completed work will do more for enquiries than any layout. That is also the difference between a site that reads as generic and one that reads as trustworthy — the point we made in the comparison with running a Facebook page.

When is custom worth the money?

When the visitor has to do something unusual. Booking a specific bay for a specific vehicle, filtering by area and price, uploading a document for a quote. Templates handle browse-and-call. They handle bespoke flows badly.

When speed to enquiry decides the sale. In urgent trades, seconds and taps matter more than looks. Stripping a page down to one obvious action is design work — and it is the work that pays for itself, as we set out in the setup that actually gets plumbers called and on our page for electricians.

When you sell on brand. Wedding venues, architects, high-end salons, anyone charging a premium for taste. Here a recognisable template actively costs you money, because looking like everyone else contradicts the pitch.

When you are building on top of it for years. If the site will grow into bookings, payments or a client portal, a foundation designed for that is cheaper than three rebuilds.

What should never decide it: being told custom is "better for SEO" as a blanket statement. Search visibility comes from site speed, sensible structure, and content that answers what people search — all achievable on a good template and all easy to get wrong on a custom build. If a quote justifies its price with vague SEO claims, ask exactly what will be done. Our list of questions to ask before paying a deposit has the wording.

Frequently asked questions

Is a template website bad for SEO?

No. Google does not penalise a site for using a theme. What can hurt rankings is what often comes with a cheap template build: slow loading from unused code and heavy sliders, duplicated placeholder text left in place, and page structures that were never written around what people actually search for.

Can I start with a template and go custom later?

Yes, and for a new business it is often the sensible order. Launch something honest and fast, learn which pages people actually use and which questions they ask, then rebuild around that evidence. Just make sure you own the domain and the content so the move costs you nothing but the build.

Will people notice my website is a template?

Customers almost never recognise a specific theme. What they do notice is a site that feels generic — stock photos of people who clearly are not your team, text that could describe any business in your industry, and no proof you have done the work. Those are content problems, not template problems.

What is a hybrid website build?

A hybrid build starts from an existing framework or theme and then customises the parts that matter commercially — usually the home page, the main service page and the enquiry flow — while leaving standard pages close to the original. It is the most common approach for South African small business sites.
