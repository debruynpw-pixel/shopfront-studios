How to Compare Website Quotes Without Getting Burned

By PW de Bruyn 4 August 2026 7 min read

You sent the same WhatsApp message to three people. One came back with R7,500, one with R24,000, one with R68,000. Nobody explained why. The instinct is to assume two of them are trying it on — but usually all three are quoting honestly for three different things, and the job in front of you is to work out which thing you actually need.

Short answer

Do not compare headline prices. Write down six line items — pages, copywriting, photography, revisions, hosting and domain, and post-launch support — then ask each quoter to price those same six. Add any exclusions back into the cheaper quotes. The order usually changes once every quote covers identical work.

Why do quotes for the same website differ so much?

"Website" is not a product. It is a category, the way "vehicle" is a category — a bakkie and a delivery truck both move goods, and quoting one against the other tells you nothing useful.

When three South African studios read the same brief, they picture three different builds. One pictures a template with your logo, your photos and eight hours of work. One pictures a custom layout, an interview to get the words right, and a fortnight. One pictures all of that plus booking, payments, and a month of tuning after launch. All three then send you a number with no picture attached, which is where the confusion starts.

Underneath the spread, four variables do most of the damage:

Who writes the words. Copywriting is often the single largest hidden line. A quote that assumes you email over the text is quoting a layout job. It is not dishonest — but you will spend a weekend writing, or pay someone later.

Template versus custom. A theme-based build starts from something that already exists. Custom starts from nothing. The gap between them is mostly hours, and hours are the whole cost of a website.

What happens after launch. Some quotes end at go-live. Others include a support arrangement. A cheaper build with no support is not cheaper if you need changes in March.

Year two. Hosting, domain renewal and SSL are frequently included for twelve months and then invoiced. Two quotes can be identical in year one and R4,000 apart in year two.

How do you normalise three quotes?

Normalising means forcing every quote to describe the same job. It takes about twenty minutes and it is the only step that matters.

Step one: write the brief down once. Number of pages. What each page is for. Whether you need bookings, payments, a gallery, a quote form. Where enquiries must land — email, WhatsApp, or both. Send the identical text to everyone. Most owners skip this and describe the job slightly differently three times, which guarantees three incomparable answers.

Step two: demand the same six line items. Ask each quoter to break the number into: build, copywriting, images, revisions, hosting and domain for year one and year two, and support after launch. If someone refuses to itemise, that is data about how the project will run.

Step three: add the exclusions back. This is where orders flip. If the R7,500 quote excludes copy and you would need eight pages written, add a realistic figure for that. If it excludes hosting from month thirteen, add a year.

Step four: check the deliverable is a business asset, not a picture. Ask each one what happens when someone lands on the site at 19:00 on a Sunday. If the answer is "they can see your services," that is a brochure. If the answer is "they tap a button and a WhatsApp message opens with your name in it," that is a lead. Same page count, completely different value — the mechanic we broke down in the website setup that actually gets plumbers called.

Run that exercise and one of three things becomes true. Sometimes the cheap quote stays genuinely cheapest and you should take it. Sometimes the middle quote turns out to be the same job as the expensive one minus the photoshoot. And sometimes the cheap quote was never quoting your project at all.

One caution about the top end: a bigger number is not proof of a better outcome. A twelve-page site is not automatically better than an eight-page one — for most service businesses it is worse, because the visitor has more places to get lost before they find the phone number. Pay for scope you can name a reason for.

What should decide it once the numbers are level?

When two quotes end up genuinely comparable, price stops being the useful signal. What predicts a good outcome is much more boring than that.

Who asked you better questions. The designer who asked what your average job is worth, where your customers come from, and what you want the site to do is going to build you something different from the one who asked how many pages you want. Quality of questions in the quoting stage is the cheapest available predictor of quality of work.

Who is clearest about ownership. Domain, hosting and files should end up in your name. If one quote states that plainly and another gets vague, that difference is worth more than a few thousand rand. We list the exact wording to ask for in 12 questions to ask a web designer before you pay a deposit.

Who understands your customer's phone. Most South African service enquiries arrive on a mid-range Android, often on mobile data, frequently after hours. A quote that mentions load time, tap targets and WhatsApp is from someone who has watched that happen. If you are in a trade, the sector-specific version of this matters — it is why we keep separate notes on what plumbers' sites need versus everyone else's.

Who will still be there in a year. Ask for two clients whose sites went live more than six months ago and phone them. It takes ten minutes and it is the most informative ten minutes in the whole process.

Finally, do not let a quote expire into a decision. "This price is only valid today" is a sales tactic, not a schedule. Any studio worth hiring will hold a number for a week while you compare properly.

Frequently asked questions

How many website quotes should I get?

Three is enough. Two gives you no sense of the middle, and beyond four you are mostly delaying the decision. What matters far more than the number of quotes is that all three were given the same written brief — otherwise you are comparing three different websites, not three prices.

Why are website quotes in South Africa so different from each other?

Because the word website covers everything from a one-page template with your logo dropped in to a custom-built, copywritten, search-optimised site with booking and payment integration. Quotes also differ on who writes the words, who supplies photos, how many revisions are included, and whether year-two hosting is in the price.

Should I always choose the cheapest website quote?

No, and not because cheap means bad. The cheapest quote is often cheapest because it excludes copywriting, photography or support that you will have to pay for anyway. Normalise the quotes first by adding those exclusions in, then choose. Sometimes the cheapest quote is still cheapest, and then it is genuinely the right call.

What should I do if a quote has no breakdown?

Ask for one in writing before you compare it to anything. A single number with no line items cannot be evaluated, and it also tells you how the project will be run. If a designer will not itemise what you are buying, expect the same vagueness about deadlines and scope once work starts.
